How the reading becomes VRN

An acoustic estimate from your mic, in VRN (VSV c06). It reads the sound, not the sensation — a mirror, not a verdict.

[O]Oral — bright, forward, high F2
[H]Head — lifted, strong upper formants
[C]Chest — low, dark, low formants + F0
[Sq]Squillo — energy in the 2.8–3.2 kHz ring
[Ch+]/[Sc]Chiaro (bright) / Scuro (dark)
[F1↑][F2↓]Formant shift vs the target vowel
// example — a bright, forward /i/ might render as [O++, H++, Sq+, Ch+] "/i/ (heed)"

What the mic can and can't see: formants (F1–F3), pitch, and the squillo ring are directly measurable, so [O] [H] [C] [Sq] [Ch+] [Sc] [F1↑] [F2↓] are grounded. VRN's muscular / sensation symbols — [D] [Ap] [Zy] [Cov] [N] — can't be read from audio; they're the singer's felt input. This tool fills the acoustic half of a VRN line and leaves the body half to you.

Why it's different for every person

A formant is a resonance of your vocal tract, and resonance frequency scales with tract length. A longer tract (typically lower/male voices) pushes every formant down; a shorter tract (higher voices, children) pushes them up. The same vowel /i/ can read ~270 Hz F1 for a bass and ~440 Hz for a child — same vowel, different numbers. The relative layout of the vowel space is shared; the absolute Hz are yours. That's why the voice-type selector rescales the reference vowels — and why serious tools let you calibrate to your own voice.

Desktop apps that measure this for real (deeper than a browser demo): Praat (free, LPC formants)VoceVista Sing&SeeSygyt / Overtone Analyzer They use longer, cleaner analysis windows, so their numbers are steadier — but the method here (LPC spectral-envelope peak-picking) is the same idea.
